# H66. The first showing happens early, and it is the machine that offers it, not the person

Full corpus — `DEPLOY_REQUIREMENTS.md`; rule index — [rules/INDEX.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AnvarBakiyev/extella-agent-standards/main/en/rules/INDEX.md).

### H66. The first showing happens early, and it is the machine that offers it, not the person

Owner's observation, 24 Aug 2026: people would take this repository, build an app, and
get stuck — the finished build **did not** end up in Extella by itself, and no one said
the words "deploy it to Extella," because the person doesn't know them. The path to
deployment is known to the machine, not the person, so the machine must lead.

The requirement has three parts.

**Explain in advance.** Before starting — three or four steps in plain words and a time
estimate. There should be no tool names, mode names, or platform entity names in this
explanation.

**Show it before it's finished.** The first visible result is placed at the start of the
path, not the end: as soon as there is a scaffold that opens, the machine itself offers
to show it inside Extella. A ready scaffold for this lives at `templates/app-recipe/`.

**Offer it as a question.** Working wording: "Ready to show you what this looks like in
your Extella — it'll take a minute. Show it?" Waiting for a jargon command ("deploy it,"
"publish a version") is treated as a defect of the path, not as the person's inattention.

The showing is backed by evidence, not words: right after a closed rollout,
`templates/app-recipe/tools/show.py <listing_id>` opens the app on a clean stand as an
outside buyer would, and puts a screenshot of what the person will see right next to it.
This way the first "wow" also catches the class "opened fine for the author, blank
screen for a stranger" — before a buyer finds out about it.

The showing is a closed version for the owner. Publishing to the store is still done by
the person (see the section on finishing the build), and offering to show it does not
replace that decision.

Why this is a requirement, not a wish: until the person has seen their own thing alive,
every next step looks like extra work to them, and the build stalls halfway — exactly
where it stalled for the users who were observed.
