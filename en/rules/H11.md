# H11. Purchases are readable — but not from the page (verified 12 Aug 2026)

Full corpus — `DEPLOY_REQUIREMENTS.md`; rules index — [rules/INDEX.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AnvarBakiyev/extella-agent-standards/main/rules/INDEX.md).

### H11. Purchases are readable — but not from the page (verified 12 Aug 2026)

Verified with live requests: `GET /api/my-purchases` and `GET /api/my-listings` **exist and
respond 200**. The app page is served from the same domain as the API (`os.extella.ai`), meaning
there is no wall between them.

One thing is missing: **these endpoints only recognize the account token**, and you can't hand
them an `app_token`.

**CORRECTION on the same day, after guidance from the CTO.** I wrote "`{{token}}` is forbidden to
use." More precisely: it is **fully privileged and expensive**, not forbidden. The platform
supports it deliberately — before the first opening of the OS it shows the buyer a "Data access"
consent dialog (with a "don't ask again" option), and the platform author's own recommendation
matches ours: don't hand it out unless strictly necessary, use `{{app_token}}` for your own agent
(H12).

Rule: `{{token}}` only when the product needs **more than its own agent** (our case — managing a
fleet of agents), and then an honestly disclosed consent dialog is part of the UX. In every other
case — it's a defect.

**What to do until the platform improves this.** The source of "what the user has" is not
purchases, but **core objects**: agents, their experts, rules, devices. Their adapter expert reads
this today, and that's enough for an automation control panel. Purchases are an enrichment
("delivered from the store, this version"), not a foundation. If a field is missing — say so in
the interface, don't hide it.

**Platform responses on 13 Aug 2026 to our requests:** the listing icon on the web-app shortcut —
**will be fixed** ("I'll fix it quickly"); deleting your own purchase — **hiding is planned**,
with a return via the store rather than deletion. Until then our own test products stay in the
launcher: there's no purchase-deletion API, and the listing isn't deleted while the purchase
exists.

**Request to the platform — partly closed the same day.** App permissions (H12) gave the page
lawful, narrow reads: `expert.read`, `kv.read`, `rules.read`, with consent and revocation. What
remains unclosed is only enumerating **purchases** by `app_token` — and enumerating **other
people's agents**, which is closed by design given the model.

### Acceptance order for a page-type product

0. **`app_scopes` are declared and minimal** (H12). If empty, the app cannot call the agent at
   all, and this only shows up on a live call, not at publish time. The device-side part needs
   `device.run`;
1. the source's `agent/run` responds without `pro_key_required` (if the agent is attached);
2. publishing returns `stats` on the product's composition, `page` is accepted, the listing is a
   pre-release;
3. `GET /app-page/{lid}` returns the page, not a 404 "This app has no web page";
4. `{{token}}`/`{{agent_id}}` were substituted in the opened page, and there are **no** leftover
   placeholders in the visible text;
5. one product scenario runs live from start to finish.

Never having passed this — the first run will be the proof, not a formality.

### What follows from this for our own products

**CORRECTION on 12 Aug 2026 to this section.** Below it used to say that the Recruiter couldn't be
moved to `page` "because there's nothing to call the expert with from the sandbox." This is
**wrong** — H5 and H5-bis proved the opposite the same day. I'm leaving the wording corrected
rather than deleting it: two chats used exactly this phrase to justify stopping work.

**The Recruiter** can have a page-type surface, and the only question now is the cost of the call:
its screens are built on frequent calls to `rec_*`, and each call from the page costs 13–17
seconds. It's worth moving it to a hybrid (H10) and only after rewriting the adapter to a
call-snapshot.

**The telecom operator pack** stays device-type for two reasons, and neither is about
possibility: 93 MB (the `page` limit is 20 MB compressed) and real client data that must not
leave the machine.

**What's worth making page-type is whatever logic lives in the browser or in the OS API** —
desktop control panels, report storefronts, builders, attachment viewers via "Open with…".

## Before Publish: acceptance is mandatory, and gates don't replace it (calibration, 12 Aug 2026)

Three chats in a row brought the owner to the Publish button right after green gates — meaning
the instructions were silent, not that the chats were wrong. Rule:

**Green gates do not authorize publishing.** Before Publish, acceptance is mandatory:

1. publish as a **pre-release** (visible only to the author) — not straight into the store;
2. buy it for yourself;
3. **first run in a clean state** — as a buyer would see it, with none of the owner's configs or
   keys: onboarding greets you, everything "not connected" is stated in words, not as errors;
4. only after this — Publish, and a person clicks it.

**The boundary of client keys.** A product has two kinds of external dependencies, and confusing
them is a mistake in both directions:

| | Ours (platform, experts, bridge, install) | The client's (a Tourvisor key, a WhatsApp number, a CRM) |
|---|---|---|
| Acceptance requires | working live | the product **is honest without them**: it leads to the key field, empty states stated in words |
| Is their absence a blocker? | yes | **no** — this is a designed state |

An "end-to-end live scenario" for a product with client keys means the scenario **up to the key's
boundary**. Requiring someone else's live key before publishing is the same mistake as requiring a
passport from a prototype.

## Finishing the build: the chat gets it to pre-release, the person clicks Publish (ed. evening of 12 Aug 2026)

The first edition said "the chat prepares the artifact, the person uploads it" — and this created
a bottleneck: the chat would hit the ceiling of "zip + instructions," and all acceptance would
queue up for the person. In one day four products out of five didn't reach deployment for exactly
this reason.

We separate two different actions that used to share the single word "publication":

| Action | Who | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Publish as a **pre-release** (`published=0`) | **the chat, on its own** | a pre-release is visible to nobody but the author: no risk |
| **Buying it for yourself** and installing | **the chat, on its own** | this is a check, not a distribution |
| First run in a clean state, the scenario | **the chat, on its own**; the person watches with their own eyes | acceptance |
| **Publish to the store** (`published=1`) | **only the person** | visible to everyone; REVERSIBLE — undone at the same address with `{"published": false}` (H26) |

**The target handoff state:** "the pre-release is published, bought for myself, installed, the
first clean-state run passed, the scenario ran live — one Publish click remains." The chat must
get exactly to this point and attach the evidence: `listing_id`, `version_id`, what the first run
showed, what didn't line up.

The artifact (zip) still gets placed at the named location regardless — it's needed for
republishing and for the case when the OS's own form turns out more convenient than the API.
