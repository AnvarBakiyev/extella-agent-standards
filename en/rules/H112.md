### H112. THE AGENT'S ENTRY POINT AND THE APP FOR HUMANS DO NOT DIVERGE

**Measurement, 25 Sep 2026.** One and the same procedure is described twice, for two different
readers: `AGENT_START.md` is read by the chat agent, the "Development on Extella" app — by the
human. Pairs like this drift apart silently and always in the same direction: whichever one is
at hand gets edited. The cost was measured on billed client work: two external chats got stuck on
facts that were present in one place and absent in the other.

The same day turned up a direct consequence: in the app's English text the expert was named
`dev_connect_agent`, in the Russian one — `dev_connect_assistant`. No expert with the first name
exists; an English-reading reader got "Expert not found." The translation fingerprints matched,
the file names matched, and the check stayed silent — it only cross-checked file names and links.

**Rule.**

1. The app's section on pitfalls declares the list of rules it covers (`entry_rules`). Every rule
   from the pitfalls section must be in this list, and be present in the entry point. The reverse
   is not required — the app is free to say more.
2. The prompt in the app leads to the same entry point (`AGENT_START.md`), gives raw links, and
   states outright that the public reference `api.html` is not the source of truth.
3. **Expert and module names are translation anchors.** They must not be translated "by meaning";
   they are cross-checked on the same footing as file names and links.
4. **A retelling leads to the source.** A section of the app that repeats the entry point must give
   the entry point's raw address: a retelling goes stale sooner than the source, and reaching the
   source must take one step, not a search. In the canon keeper's words: fewer copies means less
   work for the check.

Held in place by `tools/check_entry_app_agreement.py` and the extended anchors in
`tools/check_translation.py`.

**A second copy of the prompt was also removed along the way.** It sat both in `content.json` and
as an array of strings in the page template; the two copies had already drifted apart twice (H81,
H110). Now the button reads the same section the person sees on screen — one source, and the text
refreshes from the repository without redeploying the page.
