# H61. The rate limit: parallelism does not get around it, it makes things worse

Full corpus — `DEPLOY_REQUIREMENTS.md`; rule index — [rules/INDEX.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AnvarBakiyev/extella-agent-standards/main/en/rules/INDEX.md).

### H61. The rate limit: parallelism does not get around it, it makes things worse

**Probe from the same chat, 13–14 Aug 2026.** Reading the fleet made about 97 calls to
the core for 48 agents. The limit is **120 requests per 60 seconds**. Parallelism of
eight did not speed the work up and worsened the average time: the check took about
26 seconds and ran into a rate-limit refusal, after which the retry started over from
the ones already checked.

**What to do.** Keep parallelism below the limit, with margin. After a rate-limit
refusal, do not launch a new batch. Save confirmed results and, on retry, continue only
with the unchecked ones. Show the person the stated wait time and a countdown, not a
silent wait. Read the initial listing as a short list, details on request.

**The general point.** A rate-limit refusal is not a failure but a normal state: it
comes with a stated time after which it will pass. A refusal with a known time must
show that time, otherwise the person hits retry and extends their own limit.

**A related case, from the same source.** Revoked or never-granted permissions give the
same feeling of an empty product: the interface loads in full, and every request
answers with an access refusal, and the list stays empty. An access refusal is presented
as a recoverable state: name the required permission and the action, and don't turn the
last confirmed snapshot into zeros.
