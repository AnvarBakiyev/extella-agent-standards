# H16. A device target is a credential, not an identifier

Full corpus — `DEPLOY_REQUIREMENTS.md`; rules index — [rules/INDEX.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AnvarBakiyev/extella-agent-standards/main/en/rules/INDEX.md).

### H16. A device target is a credential, not an identifier

**Correction, 14 Aug 2026.** I claimed that someone else's device is unreachable. The owner
corrected me, and he's right: **if a person gave you their target, you can run your own experts on
their machine**; and with someone else's API token you can act on behalf of their whole account.

Confirmation from our own history: colleagues' tasks once **ran on the owner's machine** — the
panels weren't pinning work to their own device, and someone else's contract ended up being read on
someone else's Mac.

Hence a rule that matters more than the argument itself:

> **A target ID is treated as a secret.** Giving out a target means giving the right to run code on
> your own machine. It doesn't go into logs, chats, repositories, or product cards, any more than a
> token does.

And a consequence for future exchange between users: an "Extella ↔ Extella" channel is built on
exactly this — and exactly for this reason it requires explicit, revocable consent from both
sides, not "just send me your id."
