# H27. WHERE A PAGE-TYPE PRODUCT KEEPS ITS WORK: with us, not in the browser

Full corpus — `DEPLOY_REQUIREMENTS.md`; rule index — [rules/INDEX.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AnvarBakiyev/extella-agent-standards/main/rules/INDEX.md).

### H27. WHERE A PAGE-TYPE PRODUCT KEEPS ITS WORK: with us, not in the browser

**The owner's words, 16 Aug 2026:** "shouldn't this be decided by our own experts? Embed
experts, write the storage rules into them." Correct, and it removes half the question
aimed at the platform: a page in the sandbox has no browser storage, but it does have an
`app_token`, which means it has an expert and KV.

**The rule.** A page-type product does NOT store state in the browser. Anything that must
survive the window closing is written by our own expert (as a file on the buyer's device)
or into KV. `localStorage` is unavailable in the sandbox, and relying on it means losing
the person's work silently.

**Cost, measured on 16 Aug 2026** (core directly; going through the OS gateway adds
another hop):

| | |
|---|---|
| KV: write | 1.8–3.0 s |
| KV: read | ~1.4 s |
| KV: value size ceiling | around 50 KB; a 52 KB JSON came back with **HTTP 500** three times, unexplained |
| an expert on the device | 8–17 s |
| browser storage | milliseconds, but closed off in the sandbox |

**What follows from this, by product type:**

* **rare, small saves** (a log entry, settings, a report) — KV or an expert, and this is
  the right path: the work is visible to the agent and survives the browser;
* **editors and drawing tools** (tens to hundreds of KB, continuous saving) — KV doesn't
  fit, neither by size nor by cost. Here a file on the device through your own expert is
  the way, written no more often than once every few seconds;
* **someone else's app that can only do `localStorage`** — replace the storage with your
  own (`templates/storage_shim.html`), rather than relying on the browser.

**Why KV costs this much — found out on 16 Aug 2026.** At a 64 KB value, the refusal
finally named a reason: `Embedding error`. That is, **`kv/set` computes an embedding for
every value** — our KV is not "key-value," it's a vector store. Hence the second and a
half to two seconds per operation, and the ceiling around 50 KB: you can't put app state
in there, not by design malice, but by the mechanism itself.

Probe after the OS was sped up 74x (evening of 16 Aug 2026): write 2.0–2.2 s, read
1.37–1.44 s — **unchanged**. The speed-up touched the shell, not the KV core; the rules
above still stand.

**What to ask the platform for:** a KV mode without embedding — a plain key-value for
state. The CTO is in fact splitting KV into open (agent memory) and closed (input via
the OS interface); the direct question to him is whether the open mode computes an
embedding.

**A defect worth naming:** a value over the limit returns `500`, not "too large." A
silent refusal on save is the worst kind of refusal.
