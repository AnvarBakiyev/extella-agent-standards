# H83. A recipe reproduces the skill, but not the contract

Full corpus — `DEPLOY_REQUIREMENTS.md`; rule index — [rules/INDEX.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AnvarBakiyev/extella-agent-standards/main/en/rules/INDEX.md).

### H83. A recipe reproduces the skill, but not the contract

**Trial, 26 Aug 2026.** Two independent agents built the "contract reading" module from one
recipe, without seeing the finished implementation. On the same contract:

| | sample | agent A | agent B |
|---|---|---|---|
| fields of 7 | 6 | 7 | 7 |
| text sent out | no | no | no |
| passed the module check | yes | yes | yes |
| **keys in the response** | **16** | **22** | **19** |
| `file` field | present | present | **absent** |

**The capability reproduced fully**, and both third-party builds turned out better than the
sample: the agents refined the model's task on their own and got the seventh field. **The
response shape did not reproduce**: a different number of keys, different wording on three
fields, one build missing `file`. An app written against one build breaks on the other — and
that is exactly what a buyer counts as a product breakage.

**The norm.** A module's check must pin down the **response shape**, not just behavior: the
list of required keys is checked against a live response. Verified by running it backward:
under the new rule, the build without `file` turns red, the sample build passes.

From this follows a second point: **an imprint is mandatory.** It is the contract the buyer
receives; the recipe gives the skill, and sameness comes from the imprint.

**A caveat about the trial's cleanliness, important for the conclusions.** The exact value of
the consent parameter matched across all three not because the recipe sets it — it doesn't. It
is named in the passport and in the check, sitting in the same folder. So the measurement
showed the reproducibility of THREE PARTS together, not of one recipe. For the market the
framing is correct — the buyer gets all three — but the claim "the prompt alone is enough" is
not confirmed by this trial, and cannot be.

## Closing out the build: earlier revision (kept for history)

A lesson from a telecom operator's rollout on 11 Aug: the chat "prepared the rollout," but it
only got deployed after the owner asked it to assemble an archive and uploaded it by hand
through the publishing form.

Norm: **the build's output is a finished file** (a page bundle zip or a device-product archive)
in a named location, plus a three-line instruction — which fields to fill in the publishing
form and where to click. A human uploads it and presses "Publish."

Why this way, and not publishing via the API: the form works and takes a minute; publishing via
API trips up the chats (our own experience); and the button that publishes to the store is
pressed by a human — an accidental publish is ruled out by construction.
