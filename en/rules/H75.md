# H75. Visual acceptance runs at the size of the real app window

Full corpus — `DEPLOY_REQUIREMENTS.md`; rule index — [rules/INDEX.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AnvarBakiyev/extella-agent-standards/main/rules/INDEX.md).

### H75. Visual acceptance runs at the size of the real app window

**Measured 22 Aug 2026.** A product page passed acceptance in a 551×860 browser and shipped
to the store. The owner opened the real app window — three times wider — and hit three
defects at the threshold: the button overlapped the caption (a negative margin on the caption
next to a button row with no bottom), the story title broke word-by-word onto separate lines,
canvas labels ran together and got clipped. The cause of all three hid from the narrow window:
a media rule collapsed the grid into a single column, and in it everything looked healthy.

A separate lesson inside: **max-width inside max-width doesn't work.** The story block was
given its own 1120 inside a container of 760 — a child limit wider than the parent is dead
code, and it silently means the "wide" layout's design was never once exercised. A spot like
that isn't a minor thing, it's a sign: nobody has ever seen the wide layout.

**The norm.** Visual acceptance is done at a minimum of two sizes: the width of the real app
window (today that's ~1700 logical pixels) and a narrow window (~550). A green narrow run
says nothing about the wide one: they exercise different CSS branches. Horizontal overflow is
checked by a number (`scrollWidth − clientWidth === 0`), not by eye.
